# 2D → Live Action

Use this route for hand-drawn anime/donghua, manhua/manga, cel-shaded art, painterly illustration, 2D game art, concept art, and flattened AI illustration.

## Core problem

2D artwork omits or stylizes information that a photograph requires. The task is therefore **structural reconstruction**, not simple texture replacement.

Reconstruct real-world anatomy, depth, garment construction, materials, lighting, and optics while preserving the character's design language.

## 1. Face translation

Do not copy anime geometry literally and do not replace it with a generic beautiful face.

Translate these source cues into realistic human equivalents:

- face length-to-width impression;
- chin sharpness / roundness;
- cheekbone prominence;
- jaw softness / angularity;
- eye direction and emotional intensity;
- brow angle and density;
- nose delicacy / strength;
- lip fullness and expression;
- apparent adult age and temperament.

### Eye rule

Large 2D eyes represent emphasis, not literal anatomy. Reduce them to plausible adult human proportions while preserving:

- eye color;
- upward/downward tilt impression;
- eyelid character;
- gaze direction;
- calm, cool, heroic, gentle, playful, melancholic, etc. expression.

Avoid doll-like contact-lens eyes.

## 2. Hair reconstruction

A 2D hairstyle is usually a graphic silhouette. Preserve that silhouette but rebuild it as physical hair.

Preserve:

- color family;
- total length;
- bangs/fringe layout;
- parting;
- tied sections, braids, buns, ponytails;
- signature side locks;
- ornaments and ribbons.

Translate painted hair masses into:

- believable strand groups;
- fine flyaways;
- gravity and weight;
- root direction;
- translucency at edges;
- natural variation instead of one solid colored shell.

Do not turn dramatic anime hair into a hard plastic wig.

## 3. Body and pose reconstruction

Keep the source pose and body-language intent, but resolve impossible or underspecified anatomy into a plausible real pose.

Preserve:

- torso orientation;
- shoulder line;
- head tilt;
- hand action;
- leg stance;
- weight-bearing side;
- gesture rhythm;
- interaction with props/environment.

Correct only what must be corrected for real anatomy. Do not use anatomical correction as an excuse to invent a new pose.

## 4. Costume reconstruction

Treat painted costume shapes as a garment-design specification.

Infer realistic:

- garment layers;
- seams and panels;
- closures;
- collars and necklines;
- sleeve construction;
- armor attachment;
- belts/sashes;
- embroidery placement;
- fabric thickness and drape;
- footwear construction.

Preserve visible design motifs and color blocking.

For guofeng/xianxia/wuxia:

- use silk, gauze, brocade, linen, leather, metal, jade, lacquer, embroidery, woven trim, etc. where visually appropriate;
- translucent layers should remain physically plausible;
- avoid cheap shiny polyester and Halloween-costume construction;
- keep fantasy forms premium and film-production-ready rather than simplifying them into ordinary hanfu.

## 5. Prop reconstruction

A painted sword remains the same sword concept, not a random katana or generic fantasy blade.

Preserve:

- object category;
- silhouette;
- scale;
- dominant materials/colors;
- location and grip;
- iconic ornamentation.

Translate decorative shortcuts into buildable real props rather than deleting them.

## 6. Depth and environment reconstruction

2D scenes often compress perspective or use painterly atmosphere. Reconstruct a plausible physical set while preserving the layout.

Keep:

- subject placement;
- major foreground/background objects;
- horizon/elevation impression;
- dominant color relationship;
- weather/time-of-day;
- light direction;
- environmental narrative.

Add photographic depth cues:

- realistic atmospheric perspective;
- coherent focus falloff;
- contact shadows;
- reflections;
- volumetric effects only where justified;
- physically consistent scale.

## 7. Lighting translation

Painted rim light, glow, and face fill often violate physical lighting. Preserve the visual intent but make the light sources believable.

Examples:

- painted cool rim → plausible moonlight / sky rim;
- warm face glow → lantern, sunset, fire, or warm bounce;
- magical blue artifact → localized blue emissive light affecting nearby skin/fabric;
- flat cel shading → soft directional key + restrained fill + real shadow transitions.

## 8. 2D-specific negative risks

Prevent:

- literal oversized anime eyes;
- porcelain-doll face;
- smooth featureless nose;
- hard wig-like hair chunks;
- flat painted cloth pasted onto a body;
- impossible garment layering;
- missing seams/closures;
- flattened hands or feet;
- illustration outlines remaining in the photo;
- cel-shaded shadows;
- painterly brush texture on skin;
- generic cosplay redesign;
- composition drift caused by inventing a new camera.

## 9. Success criterion

The final image should look like a high-budget live-action adaptation based on the exact 2D design, not like an anime image with realistic skin painted over it.
